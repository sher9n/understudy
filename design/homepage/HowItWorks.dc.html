What happens to every call

Four steps. None of them asks you for a label, a taxonomy or a deploy.

Step one

Your calls group themselves

A call carries a shape: the instruction with the data stripped out of it, the tools it offers, the answer format it asks for and the order of the turns. Calls that share one are one workload. Nothing to label, nothing to configure.

Step two

Your own model sets the bar

We replay one sampled call through the model you run today, twice, and read how far its two answers drift apart. That is the noise floor. A candidate has to land inside it, not inside a number we picked.

Step three

Four gates, not one score

Structure, accuracy, coverage and completeness are judged separately, each against a tolerance that follows the floor. A candidate has to clear all four. Nothing is quietly dropped for being hard.

Step four

It switches, then keeps checking

The cheapest model that clears every gate can take the workload, in one click or on its own. It is then re-tested on calls you have made since, so a model that drifts is caught on new work rather than on the run that promoted it.

The rules, in short

The parts worth knowing before you point anything at us.

The bar
One and a quarter times your own model's noise on the same calls, and never looser than three percent.
A failure
A timeout, a refusal or an answer that will not parse counts as total disagreement. It is never dropped for being awkward.
The sample
Seeded and stratified by answer length, so a run can be repeated on exactly the calls it was decided on.
The revert
A promoted model that misses on fresh calls goes back at once, and is never promoted automatically again.
Your calls
Stored request and response bodies are deleted after thirty days by default, and the window is yours to set.
