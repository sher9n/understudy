How Understudy works

Understudy sits between your app and the AI models it uses. It tests other models on your own requests, and moves a job to a better one only when your answers stay as good.

Why the name?In a theater, an understudy learns the lead actor’s part, so they can step in when needed.

You do this once

Change two lines of code

Give your app Understudy’s address and your Understudy key. Your app keeps asking for the same model, and every request goes to it until a better one is proven.

two lines change
client = OpenAI(    base_url="https://api.understudy.dev/v1",    api_key=os.environ["UNDERSTUDY_KEY"],)

Then Understudy does four things

Step 1

Understand your workloads

Understudy groups your requests by the job they do, like answering order questions or summarizing support tickets. Each job is called a workload.

Each workload gets its own best model, because a model that is good at one job can be poor at another.

Step 2

Find better model options

Other models answer a sample of your real requests, and each answer is compared with your model’s answer.

Your model sets the quality bar. Ask it the same thing twice and its answers differ a little. Another model passes only if it differs from your model no more than that, plus a small margin.

For open-ended writing like poems, or answers that vary a lot, a different answer is fine if it is at least as good. A model has to pass twice, on different requests.

Step 3

Shift traffic gradually

A model that passes starts with 5 in every 100 requests, then 25, then all of them. If answers get worse at any step, requests go back.

You choose what happens when a model passes: switch automatically (the default), ask you first, or never switch.

Step 4

Learn from real outcomes

After a switch, Understudy keeps checking. Every day it compares some answers with your model’s, and it notices when an answer didn’t work, like a request sent again or a tool that failed.

If quality slips, requests go back to your model. By default, it tests again after 30 days, looking for newer, cheaper models.

Try it on your own requests

Nothing changes until a better model is proven on your requests.
